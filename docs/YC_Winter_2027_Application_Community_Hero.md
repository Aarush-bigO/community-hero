### 3. What is your company going to make? (The Story & The Product)

> Every city on Earth today is completely blind and deaf. 
> 
> You can order food to your doorstep in 12 minutes, but if an underground gas line ruptures, an electrical transformer explodes, or a flash flood washes away a road, modern cities still run on technology from 1995. Panicked citizens are forced to dial emergency hotlines where they sit on hold for 15 minutes listening to automated phone trees. Or worse, cities tell them to download a clunky municipal app and fill out a 5-page form with dropdown menus. Nobody fills out a form while a street is flooding. They hang up, panic, and walk away.
> 
> Meanwhile, when a real crisis strikes, 50 people on the same block call at once. Because caller #1 says *"fire near the bank"* and caller #10 says *"explosion across from the gas station"*, obsolete 911 and 311 systems log them as separate incidents. Cities dispatch three different repair crews to the exact same spot, while another neighborhood burns with zero help. Over 40% of municipal emergency fleet fuel is wasted on duplicate dispatches.
> 
> **Community Hero is the autonomous nervous system that fixes this globally.** 
> 
> We turn chaotic cities into intelligent, real-time closed-loop networks through three multimodal layers:
> 
> 1. **Active Citizen Voice & Vision:** Anyone can call our hotline or tap our mobile app. You don’t type. You speak naturally in whatever language you speak—English, Spanish, Hindi, Japanese. In under 850 milliseconds, our Voice AI talks back with natural human empathy, extracts exact street coordinates, gauges life-safety threat, and logs a Computer-Aided Dispatch (CAD) work order. If you have a camera, one snap automatically captures GPS and reverse-geocodes the hazard.
> 
> 2. **Passive Fleet Edge AI (The Moving City Scanner):** Cities shouldn't just rely on citizens to report problems. We mount smart edge-AI camera vision modules on existing municipal vehicle fleets—public transit buses, garbage trucks, and police cruisers. As these vehicles drive their daily routes, our computer vision passively scans roads 24/7, auto-detecting potholes, illegal trash dumping, fallen powerlines, and water leaks before a citizen even notices.
> 
> 3. **The Central Brain (75-Meter Spatial Swarm Clustering):** When 40 people call about the same transformer explosion, our spatial Haversine algorithm groups every call within a 75-meter walking radius into a single master crisis ticket in under 4 milliseconds. Instead of dispatching 3 trucks, it dispatches the exact right unit, upgrades priority to Critical, and reassures callers: *"We have already mobilized Fire Unit 4. They are 3 minutes away."*
> 
> 4. **Verified Closed-Loop Field Execution & 3D Digital Twin:** Dispatched crews receive turn-by-turn work orders. A ticket cannot be closed without an uploaded "AFTER" photo verified by computer vision—ending fake government resolutions forever. City commanders watch the pulse of the entire city on a real-time 3D geospatial digital twin.

### 4. Why did you pick this idea to work on? How do you know people need what you're making?

> We didn’t come up with this in a brainstorming session. We built it because we were furious at how helpless ordinary people are when basic infrastructure fails. 
> 
> A few months ago, a commercial transformer blew up near a crowded street. Sparks were raining on pedestrians. People were screaming, pulling out their phones, trying to call emergency hotlines. Nobody could get through—lines were busy. One guy tried to open the city’s official grievance app, clicked through three English dropdowns asking for "Ward Number" and "Sub-district Code", swore, and closed the app. Three separate fire trucks showed up 45 minutes later because dispatchers logged three separate calls. 
> 
> That day, we realized something insane: **the physical world is collapsing in real time, but our cities are being run on glorified Excel sheets and telephone switchboards.**
> 
> We talked to city workers, 911 dispatchers, and residents across multiple cities. Every single dispatcher told us the exact same nightmare: *"During a storm or crisis, 50% of our calls are duplicates from people standing on the same street corner, and our software can't tell them apart."* 
> 
> We locked ourselves in a room during the Bolna x YC Symphony Buildathon and built the first working prototype in 3 weeks. When we tested sub-850ms voice triage against real audio recordings of panicked callers, it didn’t just work—it felt like magic. We knew right then that this isn't a university project; this is how every city on Earth will operate in ten years.

### 8. How will you get your first 100 cities? (Distribution Strategy)

> 1. **The "Crisis Season" Trojan Horse Pilot:** We do not participate in 2-year government RFP bidding wars. Instead, we offer city commissioners a zero-risk, 60-day pilot for their single most congested district during peak emergency seasons (e.g., monsoon floods in Asia, winter storms in the US Northeast). We prove our value with one undeniable metric: *"We reduced your duplicate dispatches by 40% and cut emergency call wait times from 10 minutes to zero."* Once citizens and dispatchers experience it, the city cannot turn it off.
> 2. **Open311 Drop-in Compatibility:** Because we are built natively on Open311 v2 standards, we integrate with existing municipal backends via webhooks. Zero IT headaches for the city.
> 3. **Enterprise Utility First (Land & Expand):** Private power and water utilities have commercial purchasing authority and can sign software contracts in 30 days without city council votes. We sell to power grid operators first; once our incident data is proven on their grid, we sell the full CAD platform to the city government.

### 11. What is something surprising or counterintuitive you have learned?

> The most shocking thing we discovered is that **emergency dispatch centers do not suffer from a lack of trucks or manpower—they suffer from an information compression bottleneck.** 
> 
> Over 35% of all emergency calls during a severe storm are exact duplicates from people within 50 meters of each other. But because caller A says *"tree fell near the blue house"* and caller B says *"road blocked next to the bakery"*, legacy systems treat them as two separate crises. The city sends two crews. When crew #2 arrives, they discover crew #1 is already there. Meanwhile, three blocks away, a family with a real emergency gets no help. 
> 
> Solving city emergencies isn't about buying more fire trucks. It’s about building an intelligent AI filter at the point of ingestion that turns chaotic human noise into clean, deduplicated action.
